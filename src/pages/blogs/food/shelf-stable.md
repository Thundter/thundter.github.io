outlines shelf-stable survival foods available in UK supermarkets Here is a breakdown of the items, their survival value, and typical shelf lives when stored correctly

## Grains & Carbohydrates

- Couscous | Requires only hot water | lasts 20–30 years
- Pearl Barley | Great for thickening stews | lasts 20–30 years
- White Rice | A staple that | lasts up to 30 years
- Pasta | Durable durum wheat | lasts 20–30 years
- Porridge Oats | Can be cold-soaked | lasts up to 30 years
- Instant Mashed Potato | Quick calorie-dense comfort | lasts 10+ years
- Bulgar Wheat | Fast cooking (5 min) | lasts 2–3 years

## Proteins & Pulses

- Red Split Lentils | High protein, no soaking | lasts 25–30 years
- Dried Chickpeas | High expansion factor | lasts 20–30 years
- Dried Kidney Beans | Dense protein | lasts 25–30 years
- Dried Black Beans | Similar to kidney beans | lasts 25–30 years
- Green & Brown Lentils | Keeps shape | lasts 25–30 years
- Tinned Meats (Spam/Corned Beef/Chicken/Ham/Sardines/Tuna/Mackerel) | Essential protein and fats | usually 2–5 years

## Fats, Dairy & Sweeteners

- Ghee | Shelf-stable fat for energy | lasts months to years
- Tinned Coconut Milk | Crucial healthy fats | 2–5 years
- Powdered Milk (Non-fat) | Essential calcium source | up to 25 years
- Evaporated Milk | Better taste for families | 1–2 years
- Honey | Natural antibacterial properties | indefinite
- Sugar | Preservative and energy | indefinite
- Golden Syrup | Calorie booster | several years

## Flavour, Morale & Necessities

- Marmite | Vitamin B supplement | indefinite
- Herbal Tea | Psychological anchors | years
- Gravy Granules & Stock Cubes | Essential for palatable meals | years
- Tinned Fruits & Vegetables | Vitamins and morale | 2–5 years
- Baking Essentials (Flour, Corn Flour, Baking Powder, etc) | Versatile for flatbreads | years (requires moisture protection)
- Vinegar | Self-preserving and cleaning | indefinite
- Cocoa Powder | Morale booster | years
- Weetabix | Fortified nutrients | 3–5 years (in airtight containers)

Note | The creator emphasizes that for dry goods like grains and pulses, you should ideally freeze them for 24–48 hours first to kill insect eggs, then store in mylar bags with oxygen absorbers for the longest shelf life
